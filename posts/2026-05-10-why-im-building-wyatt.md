## Early days
My first exposure to anything resembling programming was in college, and it was foisted on me by the late Dr. Jeff Wragg, chair of the Physics and Astronomy Department at the College of Charleston. We were forced to complete assignments in Mathematica and write papers in LaTex. All of it was Greek to me, but fascinating none the less. To this day, I still write resumes in LaTex, but I'm too cheap to maintain a Mathematica license, so that muscle has probably all but disappeared. 

## Vibing now
But that's exactly what happens isn't it? We work hard to hone a skill, we think we've "got" the skill, and then we stop using it, and just like that, we've lost the skill. In the early parts of my career as a data-analyst, I spent many hours writing stored procedures that were hunderds of lines long, and then later as a junior-level engineer in Rails, I'd spend just as many hours trying to write functions that were as few lines as possible. However, as my workflows have shifted heavier towards LLM-assisted programming (vibe-coding/vibe-architecting), I've noticed my ability to sit and write well formed code and test and iterate and fail and improve and debug has started to atrophy as well. I'm far less patient with the discipline than I once was.

I don't necessarily think of this as a negative, because I know that, if push came to shove, I could revert back to the old days of bespoke, hand-crafted code. However, these days I spend more time planning and focusing on system architecture, and delegate feature work to agents.

## But why blog?
Writing is a skill that I aspire to be good at. Sadly, I was probably much better when I was stretching this muscle more frequently, but it's not something I have much reason to practice. Today, most of my communication consists of a quick email (gross) or more frequently, a 1-2 sentence long slack message. Rarely do I have the need or time to write and edit long thoughts. To make matters worse (or better?), anything that *does* happen to stretch beyond the 140 chars blurb, was usually written by an LLM, either in part or in whole. Even now as I write this first post, I have to **actively fight** the urge to drop this file into Claude and have it repair my sentence fragments or split up my run-on sentences. 

## Life on the fronteir
Last January, I joined some of my best friends in a venture to build WyattERP, a hyper-vertical agentic platform, purpose built to integrate with ERP systems in the AEC and GovCon space. I'll probably spend the next few posts documenting that process, highlighting lessons learned, mistakes made, and providing updates along the way.

So that's it. This is where I'll come to stretch my fingers in a self-serving way, while also documenting the journey of building software in the new era.
